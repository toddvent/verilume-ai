Advanced Analytics training guide

How to run MMM Light, read it honestly, and turn what it is unsure about into a market test. For marketers, analysts and account managers. About 30 minutes.

Version 1 · November 2026 · Covers MMM Light, Visualize markets, the Truth Lab and the qualification check. Not yet verified on the production site; screens may differ slightly until it is. · Product doc

The idea in one line. The model finds patterns in your history and tells you where it is least sure. A test then settles those questions with cause, not pattern. Then the result informs the next decision.

1. The loop

  1. Check readiness. Is there enough dated history and spend variation to model?
  2. Run MMM Light. See what it credits, with ranges and a confidence label.
  3. Pick what to test. The Test next card lists channels the model is least sure about that can be aimed by market.
  4. Plan the test. Visualize markets sizes it against your own markets and says how many weeks it needs.
  5. Run and confirm. Track it in the Truth Lab. A person decides: ship, kill or iterate.
  6. Refresh. Re-run on a schedule you choose.

2. Before you start: the qualification check

On the MMM Light page choose Run the check. It compares what is loaded with what the model and a market test need, marks each item Ready, Thin or Missing, and writes the list of what to ask the client for. It is read-only and uses no credits.

WhatNeededWhy
History52 complete weeks of dated records minimum; two years reads bestThe model must separate seasons from media.
OutcomeTransactions or revenue by booking date, with cancellations markedThis is what the model explains. Leads and direct sales calls are optional extras.
SpendSpend by channel with flight dates. Weekly actuals are better than monthly totalsThe model learns from week-to-week moves.
ChannelsAt least two (three is better), each active 26 weeks with about 25% change in weekly spendA channel at a flat level cannot be separated from the base.
MarketsA postal code on each transaction, and enough volume in test and control marketsNeeded to place bookings in markets and to see a realistic lift.

Channels that always move together, such as two bought in the same flights, cannot be told apart. The check names them. Staggering one of them for a few weeks fixes this.

3. Run MMM Light

  1. Choose what the model should explain: transactions (the usual start), revenue, leads or direct sales calls. The card shows weeks of daily history and whether it is ready.
  2. Choose Run. The button shows the credits it uses and how many you have left. A run takes about a minute.
  3. Read the result in this order: confidence and holdout, then the ranges, then the suggested move.

What a run shows

ItemHow to read it
ConfidenceHigh, Medium or Low. Based on how well the model predicted the last 13 weeks it had not seen. Low means the model did not beat the average, so no recommendations are shown.
RangesEvery number comes with a range from re-fitting on reshuffled errors. Treat the range as a guide, not a guarantee.
Media shareThe least certain number. A channel that runs every week looks like base. Do not plan from it alone.
Next dollarWhat one more dollar in each channel is expected to add. More reliable than the share.
Response curvesShow where each channel flattens. Diminishing returns: each extra dollar buys less.
Suggested moveA reallocation limited to cutting no channel below half of current spend, lifting none above one and a half times, and never above 125% of the highest weekly spend you have actually run.
Patterns, not proof. The model reads this account's own history. A price change, a competitor or weather that moved at the same time can be mistaken for media. Test the large moves before committing budget.

4. Credits and keeping it fresh

A run uses credits from the account's monthly allowance, five by default, and only when the model fits. A run that cannot fit is not charged. Enterprise accounts are unmetered.

An admin, the CMO or a Director can set the refresh under Keep it fresh: manual, monthly, every two weeks or weekly; which outcomes; and a credit reserve (20 by default) below which refreshing pauses. Larger clients with more media usually benefit from a faster cadence, smaller accounts from monthly or by hand. A scheduled refresh skips when there are fewer than seven new days of data, the outcome is not ready, or a run is already going. Scheduled runs are labelled Scheduled run.

5. From the model to a market test

  1. On the Test next card, each row is a channel the model is least sure about that can be aimed by market. The last column is what the model expects 20% more weekly spend to add, with its range.
  2. Choose Plan this test. Visualize markets opens with that channel and expected lift loaded.
  3. Read Weeks needed (pooled markets). Under 13 weeks is a standard test. Up to 26 means plan a longer test or raise the spend change. Beyond that, the markets cannot read a lift this small; use a time-based test (switch the channel up or down in the same markets and compare before and after).
  4. Choose Save as a test in the Truth Lab. The draft carries the channel, expected lift, sizing and a link to the MMM run.

Which test design fits your volume

DesignWhen
PairsThe best single test and control pair can detect the lift within 13 weeks.
PooledAll test markets as one group against all control markets as one. Single pairs are too small, pooled groups can see the lift in 13 weeks.
Pooled, longerPooled groups need about 26 weeks.
Time-basedVolume is too small for any market design. Use the markets for targeting and measure by switching media on and off.

6. Run the test and confirm

In the Truth Lab open the draft, add the test and control markets and the start date, and enter weekly bookings as the test runs. When it ends, a person confirms the result: ship, kill or iterate. Confirming teaches the Brain a lesson that appears in later recommendations. Nothing is automatic.

Not built yet. Test results are not fed back into the model to calibrate it. For now, use the result to judge whether the model's expected lift was right and to decide where to move money.

7. What MMM Light is not

  • It is not a Bayesian model with priors.
  • It is not calibrated to your test results yet.
  • It does not model markets separately, and it reads weeks, not days.
  • It works best with two or more years of history and a handful of channels whose spend changes from week to week.

Consider a deeper model when you have many channels, large budgets, steady always-on spend, heavy seasonality or promotions, or a decision that needs a tighter range. A dedicated model, calibrated with test results, will usually serve you better. Verilume can broker one from Measurement Coverage. Use MMM Light to decide where to test first.

8. When something looks wrong

You seeDo this
Not ready to model yetRead the reasons listed. Usually fewer than 52 weeks, or fewer than two channels with enough spend variation. Run the qualification check for the request list.
Confidence LowThe model could not predict recent weeks. Do not reallocate from it. Add history or spend variation, then re-run.
A channel cannot be readIt ran too few weeks or its spend barely changes. Pulse it or flight it differently for a few months.
Weeks needed is very longRaise the spend change in the test markets, choose a larger channel, or use a time-based test.
Refresh pausedCredits fell to the reserve. Raise the allowance, lower the reserve, or run by hand.
No Plan this test buttonsThe run found no channels that can be aimed by market, or the model was sure about all of them.

9. Glossary

TermMeaning
AdstockCarry-over: media keeps working after the week it runs. The half-life is the weeks for half of a week's effect to fade.
SaturationDiminishing returns: each extra dollar buys less than the one before.
HoldoutThe last 13 weeks, kept back to check whether the model predicts weeks it has not seen.
Pooled testAll test markets treated as one group, all control markets as another.
Detectable liftThe smallest lift a design could reliably see, at 95% confidence and 80% power.
