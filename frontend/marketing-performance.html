Campaign analysis & media channel comparisons

Two different comparison questions — answered as two different views, not one screen wearing two hats.

Built · live in the client portal

Comparing "everything we've run on this one channel" and "this campaign vs. that campaign" are genuinely different jobs, so they're built as genuinely different panels.

Channel Performance

Everything on one channel, ranked

Pick a channel, see every campaign that used it, ranked by that channel's own primary metric — response rate, CTR, whatever actually applies. A magazine buyer can see which of six publications actually pulls a response from one screen, not by opening six campaigns one at a time.

  • Reads real per-placement actuals where they exist, campaign-level actuals otherwise
  • Below/within/above benchmark badge, sourced from named industry benchmarks
Campaign vs. Campaign

Cross-channel, side by side

Select two or more campaigns — this quarter's launch vs. last quarter's — for a side-by-side table: spend, each campaign's own primary metric, Alignment Score, and conversions normalized per $1,000 spent, so a $2,000 campaign and a $50,000 campaign are both readable on the same axis.

  • Never forces a false shared metric across different channels or objectives
  • A benchmark verdict only appears where the comparison is actually apples-to-apples

Marketing Performance FAQ

How does budget development by timeframe work? ▸

Scoped — not yet built

The design: one annual Media Plan broken into quarterly and monthly tranches (unevenly, if the business needs it — a retail account can weight Q4 heavier without an even split). Dollars allocate two levels deep — first across the five stages of the customer journey (Awareness, Consideration, Purchase, Loyalty, Advocacy), then by channel within each stage — with a recommended starting mix driven by spend level, industry, and competitive set, always with a human override. Pacing will roll up two ways once it's built: a CMO view of the full plan, and a Director view scoped to whatever that Director owns.

Every channel a real media plan needs is covered, digital and offline alike:

Search Social Display Video Email PR / Earned Linear (TV) Direct Mail Events Podcasts Radio Magazines

What does the testing-methodology recommendation actually say, by budget tier? ▸

01

Under $5K

No formal statistical test. Sequential, one-variable learning — change one thing next time, compare wave over wave.

02

$5K–$25K

One clean A/B test on the single highest-leverage variable — usually creative or offer, not both at once.

03

$25K+

A real A/B or multivariate test with a holdout group above $25K, scaling to a structured test-and-learn program with geo holdouts above $100K.

Benchmark ranges throughout are sourced from named references (Google and Meta's own ad benchmark reports, the DMA Response Rate Report, RAB, Mailchimp/Campaign Monitor) — never presented as your account's own historical data.

What's built today for Media Mix Modeling (MMM)? ▸

Built · data layer live

Every account can log historical spend, reach, and impressions across 18 fixed categories, with campaigns feeding that ledger directly by their own start date — no manual double-entry required. A partner-ready category mapping, CSV export, and an analysis-readiness readout (time-series depth, per-category spend variation, collinearity risk) are all built and live. The actual statistical modeling is still integration-required — it needs a real MMM tool or vendor chosen, which is a business decision, not a build decision. This page will update the moment that choice is made.

Is neuroscience-informed measurement part of Verilume? ▸

Early roadmap — not scoped yet

Neuroscience-informed measurement — attention, emotional response, and other behavioral research inputs alongside the media and CX data Verilume already scores — is a direction we're tracking as a complement to MMM, not something built or even fully scoped yet. We're naming it here because it's part of where advanced analytics is headed for a CMO's stack, not to claim it exists today. No vendor, method, or timeline is set.
